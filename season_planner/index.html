Commonhold Rise

The Crown had called for settlers to a newly discovered land, and we were the crew who answered — not under a tyrant, but as a community, under one banner.

We arrive at the spawning settlement, where the court mage has graciously provided a point of Revival, such that none of us may ever truly die during our endeavors. We begin as the Crown once intended, just poorer for the journey. Nobody is a king. Nobody owns land. Nobody holds a title. We gather what the old world taught us and what little this shore provides — wood, stone, iron, food, shelter — and raise our first settlement. We call it New Haven.

Already, explorers speak of forests untouched, mountains holding ores nobody's named, deserts hiding ruins that aren't ours — a whole world past New Haven's fences, unclaimed and unwritten.

Nothing out there is scripted. Whatever Commonhold Rise becomes, we're the ones who write it — one settlement, one story, one adventure at a time.

The Commonhold Ethos

  • Nothing is automated. No iron farms, no book farms — what you have, you mined, farmed, or hunted yourself.
  • Everything is walked, ridden, packed, railed, or sailed. That's why the roads — and the small settlements along them — matter.
  • Some milestones belong to everyone. Most storylines need real turnout, not just a name on a list — the Hall of Heroes exists for exactly this, recording who took part in a raid so a win can't quietly get claimed by whoever happened to solo it. The bar is at least 4 people. Where a node doesn't have a Hall entry to check against, that same standard is a house rule the group enforces on its honor instead.

How This Season Works

We start with extreme limitations — no Nether or End access, no villager trading, no diamond tools or armor, elytra and dispensers stripped on sight, piglins and iron golems blocked outright. Through a series of events with fixed goals, we gain rewards, unlock blocked content, and progress to further stories. The stories themselves are not currently fixed — they will be created by the players. The progression tree also isn't fixed — it grows through storylines that you propose and vote on.

  • Any player can propose a storyline, at any time — it doesn't have to come from the owner.
  • A proposal needs to define the same four things every node on the tree already has: the pass criteria (what actually has to happen to complete it), the reward (what the group gets for finishing it), what it unlocks, and whether it leads into another storyline further down the tree or stands on its own.
  • Once those four things are nailed down, it goes up for a group vote.
  • If it passes, it gets added to the tree — written up in the same format as everything else.
  • Story creators can be granted temporary special privileges — given materials, creative access, structure block pasting — specifically to build out storyline content. Once that content's built, the privileges go away and they're back to being a commoner like everyone else.

Starting Restrictions

This is the initial set of things that get controlled as disabled — the starting state before any progression has happened. It's meant to change: as the group works through a dynamic, growing storyline and progression tree, this list gets updated to match.

  • Everything here applies regardless of what's been cleared elsewhere — individual nodes in the tree below only call out what's new for them, not the full standing rule set. Nothing here is permanent; the storyline isn't finished.
  • Nether portal blocked, End portal blocked
  • Removed on pickup, crafted or found — everything below except the diamond items and Honey Block: elytra, dispenser, dropper, sticky_piston, piston, conduit, hopper, hopper_minecart, crafter, observer, blaze_powder, enchanting_table, comparator, repeater, daylight_detector
  • Uncraftable, opened by planned progression to reopen: conduit
  • Uncraftable, possible future planned progression: dispenser, hopper, hopper_minecart, piston, sticky_piston, dropper, crafter, observer, blaze_powder, enchanting_table, comparator, repeater, daylight_detector, diamond_pickaxe, diamond_axe, diamond_shovel, diamond_hoe, diamond_sword, diamond_spear, diamond_helmet, diamond_chestplate, diamond_leggings, diamond_boots
  • Villager trades: all disabled (every profession + wandering trader)
  • Fishing: Bow and Fishing Rod treasure catches can come pre-enchanted again; Book catches still never do
  • Blocked entities: iron_golem, piglin, piglin_brute

The Storylines

Start — no prerequisites Complete

Founding the Camp

This is where Commonhold Rise actually begins — not with a quest, but with shelter. The ship is gone, the Crown's blessing is a name on a scroll, and what's left is a stretch of unclaimed coastline and whatever the group can build with their own hands. Before anyone touches a portal or wires a redstone contraption, New Haven needs walls: a personal space for every member within the base boundary, working crop farms, livestock pens, and a common store everyone can draw from. It's slow, deliberate work on purpose — everything the season builds toward rests on the ground laid down right here.

What actually happened: the group landed with nothing but seeds, and New Haven grew out of the farms they planted.

Pass Criteria

  • Every member has claimed and built a personal space within the base boundaryDecreed done. Members spread out a little past the boundary.
  • Working farms established for all 6 crops: wheat, carrots, potatoes, beetroot, melon, and pumpkinDone.
  • At least two livestock pens are built and stockedDone. Lots of animal farms, spread around rather than consolidated.
  • A common storage location is designated and accessible to the whole groupDone.

Unlocks

  • Contact made with the nearby village

Leads to: Hall of Heroes and Breaking Bread, which run side by side

Requires: Founding the Camp. Runs alongside Breaking Bread, and must be complete before Breaking Bread can finish. Current

Hall of Heroes

Every village New Haven backs from here on is about to get tested by fire, and someone decides those wins shouldn't just be a story people tell each other afterward. The group raises a proper Hall back at the original settlement — plaques, records, whatever passes for a monument this early in the season. Nothing that happens at any other village counts toward anything until it's written down here first.

Pass Criteria

  • A Hall of Heroes is constructed at the original settlement

Unlocks

  • A place now exists to formally record raid victories — each entry notes when the raid happened, who took part (at least 4 people — not one or two players soloing it), and, for any village found by map, displays the actual map that led there. Required before any village's raid win counts toward its trade unlock.

Leads to: Breaking Bread, Shipwreck & Treasure, The Archery Contest

Requires: Founding the Camp to start, Hall of Heroes to finish Current

Breaking Bread

The nearest village wants nothing to do with New Haven. Too many wandering bands have come through before with more interest in scavenging than settling, and right now the group looks exactly like one of them. Earning actual trust means showing up and doing something useful without asking for anything back first: a real road connecting the two settlements, and enough reinforcement — walls, lighting, whatever it takes — that the village stops losing its own people to mobs at night. Walls aren't proof, though. Bad Omen was never locked behind anything: any Illager Outpost gives up an Ominous Bottle, and there are always more outposts than raids anyone needs. So the group brings the raids to the village on purpose, three times over and at full intensity, to show the defenses hold under real pressure. Before the first raid, New Haven declares one villager the village leader and gives him a name of its own choosing. Through all of it, the leader matters more than anyone. If he falls, no amount of walls will convince anyone there that New Haven can keep them safe. Only once the village has seen New Haven stand with it does anyone there warm up at all, enough for its cleric, cartographer and fletcher to start trading, and for the cartographer to start pointing the way toward other settlements.

Pass Criteria

  • Connect the village
    • A trade route (road) connects the base to the village
  • Build out the village, as agreed in the group vote
    • Housing / hotel district (apartment complex or similar)
    • Farming district
    • Market district
    • Town hall (mayor's office)
    • Reinforcement against mob attacks (walls, lighting, or equivalent defenses)
    • Each build is claimed by one owner who leads it; anyone can help
  • Before the first raid
    • The village has at least 20 villagers
    • The group declares one villager the village leader, chooses his name, and name-tags him
  • Survive 3 maximum-level raids at the village
    • At least 4 people take part in each raid
    • No more than 2 villagers are lost across all 3 raids, counting only villagers who were there before the first raid
    • The village leader survives
  • Record it in the Hall of Heroes
    • The Hall of Heroes is complete
    • All 3 raids are recorded there, noting when each happened and who took part

Unlocks

  • Counts as the first village toward the Village Milestones
  • Cleric trades restored
  • Cartographer trades restored, village maps only
  • Fletcher trades restored

Leads to: The Mystic Library, the other village storylines, Village Milestones

Requires: Breaking Bread Story & Criteria TBD

The Mystic Library

There are no enchanting tables in New Haven. Whatever the old world knew about enchanting didn't survive the crossing intact. Once the village trusts New Haven enough to talk, its librarians admit they've kept what's left of that knowledge, but have nowhere fit to study it. Building them a proper library, a place to learn and focus, is how enchanted books come back into the world: not from a table, but from people. From then on, the community decides together which books the librarians learn next.

Pass Criteria

  • The Mystic Library is built for the librarians (design and requirements still to be written)

Unlocks

  • Mystic Library librarians start selling enchanted books: every Tier I book in General and Fishing goes on sale free of tokens. Mending is never sold.
  • The token tree opens, so higher tiers can be bought with tokens earned at events

Leads to: the Mystic Library enchanting tree, which keeps growing through the rest of the season; The Archery Contest

Requires: The Mystic Library and Hall of Heroes Story TBD

The Archery Contest

The librarians won't teach the bow books to a settlement that can't shoot straight. It's up to the players to prove otherwise: design an archery game, make it genuinely hard to pass, and hold a contest where at least half the group can still clear it.

Pass Criteria

  • Players design and build an archery game that is difficult to pass
  • The game is run as a contest event with at least 4 entrants — see the Commonhold Ethos
  • At least half of the group that enters succeeds during the contest
  • The contest is recorded in the Hall of Heroes — noting when it happened, who entered, and who passed

Unlocks

  • Ranged opens in the Mystic Library: its Tier I books can be bought for 1 token each
Requires: Trading on the Edge Story & Unlocks TBD

Pass Criteria

Unlocks

Whatever's inside is worth the trouble: raw materials the whole group can put toward the same thing, rather than one player pocketing it and moving on — and worth writing down properly, same as every other win that's gone into the Hall.

Each village after it has to be a kind the group hasn't done yet, in any order. Breaking Bread's village happens to be Plains, so the other four kinds are the storylines below.

The Mystic Library

There are no enchanting tables in New Haven. Enchanted books come from the librarians of the Mystic Library, and the community decides with tokens which books they learn next. The library itself can't be started until Breaking Bread is complete.

How the Tree Climbs

  • Building the library is its own reward. Finishing The Mystic Library storyline, which follows Breaking Bread, puts every Tier I book in General and Fishing on sale at once, free of tokens, as thanks for helping the librarians build a place of knowledge. Mending is never sold.
  • The other groups open as the season moves forward. Once the library is complete, Tools opens at 2 villages in the Village Milestones (alongside the Butcher, Farmer and Fisherman), and Armor and Melee open at 4 villages (alongside the Armorer, Toolsmith and Weaponsmith). Ranged opens with The Archery Contest, Fire with the Nether, and Water with the Ocean Monument. Each one opens that group's Tier I books for tokens.
  • Everyone who takes part earns a token. Each participant in an event or questline earns 1 token and picks which open book it goes toward. Whatever gets unlocked is unlocked for everyone.
  • Each group climbs on its own. A tier opens once enough books in the tier below it are unlocked, so one book can't race to the top alone.
  • Every book after that costs tokens. Outside General and Fishing, a Tier I book costs 1 token, Tier II costs 2, Tier III costs 3, and so on.

Unplanned Progression

Things that are real standing restrictions today but don't have a real story or milestone attached yet — parked here on purpose instead of forcing them onto a node that isn't ready. Future story planning and unlocks will be discussed and coordinated with the group, and decided by majority vote.

  • Pistons and dispensers. Banned since Founding the Camp. Used to hang off a "Witch's Hovel" idea that wasn't developed enough to commit to — cut for now. Needs a real replacement milestone.
  • Diamond gear. Tools, sword, spear, and armor all banned since Founding the Camp. Tools used to hang off Illager Outpost and the weapons/armor off a dedicated Diamond Gear node — neither exists in the tree anymore. The whole diamond kit still needs a new unlock milestone. The Armorer, Toolsmith and Weaponsmith come back at 4 villages, but without their diamond gear sales.
  • How the group first finds the Woodland Mansion. The node itself is fully built out (Pass Criteria, Unlocks, everything downstream), but the discovery hook — how they even learn it's out there — is currently just a placeholder pointing at Trading on the Edge. Needs its own story.
  • Blaze powder and early potions. Blaze powder is removed on sight, so no brewing is possible before the Nether. One idea from Fredo: a witch storyline brings basic potion brewing in early (with Weakness possibly restricted), and a Cleric trade becomes a renewable source of blaze powder. Useful potions like Water Breathing and Fire Resistance would still wait for their own milestones, such as Water Breathing after the Ocean Monument. Not a skill tree, just a storyline.
  • Comparator and Daylight Detector. Banned since Founding the Camp, same as the diamond kit — locked from day one, before the Nether even exists to get quartz from. The idea is that once The Nether Opens, quartz itself and everything built purely from Quartz Block stays fair game, but these two redstone components stay off-limits until a real milestone says otherwise.
