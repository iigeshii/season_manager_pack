Commonhold Rise

The Crown had called for settlers to a newly discovered land, and we were the crew who answered — not under a tyrant, but as a community, under one banner.

We arrive at the spawning settlement, where the court mage has graciously provided a point of Revival, such that none of us may ever truly die during our endeavors. We begin as the Crown once intended, just poorer for the journey. Nobody is a king. Nobody owns land. Nobody holds a title. We gather what the old world taught us and what little this shore provides — wood, stone, iron, food, shelter — and raise our first settlement. We call it New Haven.

Already, explorers speak of forests untouched, mountains holding ores nobody's named, deserts hiding ruins that aren't ours — a whole world past New Haven's fences, unclaimed and unwritten.

Nothing out there is scripted. Whatever Commonhold Rise becomes, we're the ones who write it — one settlement, one story, one adventure at a time.

The Commonhold Ethos

  • Nothing is automated. No iron farms, no book farms — what you have, you mined, farmed, or hunted yourself.
  • Everything is walked, ridden, packed, railed, or sailed. That's why the roads — and the small settlements along them — matter.
  • Some milestones belong to everyone. Most storylines need real turnout, not just a name on a list — the Hall of Heroes exists for exactly this, recording who took part in a raid so a win can't quietly get claimed by whoever happened to solo it. The bar is at least 4 people. Where a node doesn't have a Hall entry to check against, that same standard is a house rule the group enforces on its honor instead.

How This Season Works

We start with extreme limitations — no Nether or End access, no villager trading, no diamond tools or armor, elytra and dispensers stripped on sight, piglins and iron golems blocked outright. Through a series of events with fixed goals, we gain rewards, unlock blocked content, and progress to further stories. The stories themselves are not currently fixed — they will be created by the players. The progression tree also isn't fixed — it grows through storylines that you propose and vote on.

  • Any player can propose a storyline, at any time — it doesn't have to come from the owner.
  • A proposal needs to define the same four things every node on the tree already has: the pass criteria (what actually has to happen to complete it), the reward (what the group gets for finishing it), what it unlocks, and whether it leads into another storyline further down the tree or stands on its own.
  • Once those four things are nailed down, it goes up for a group vote.
  • If it passes, it gets added to the tree — written up in the same format as everything else.
  • Story creators can be granted temporary special privileges — given materials, creative access, structure block pasting — specifically to build out storyline content. Once that content's built, the privileges go away and they're back to being a commoner like everyone else.

Starting Restrictions

This is the initial set of things that get controlled as disabled — the starting state before any progression has happened. It's meant to change: as the group works through a dynamic, growing storyline and progression tree, this list gets updated to match.

  • Everything here applies regardless of what's been cleared elsewhere — individual nodes in the tree below only call out what's new for them, not the full standing rule set. Nothing here is permanent; the storyline isn't finished.
  • Nether portal blocked, End portal blocked
  • Removed on pickup, crafted or found: elytra, dispenser, dropper, sticky_piston, piston
  • Uncraftable, opened by planned progression to reopen: anvil, conduit
  • Uncraftable, possible future planned progression: dispenser, hopper, hopper_minecart, piston, sticky_piston, dropper, crafter, slime, honey_block, observer, blaze_powder, enchanting_table, comparator, daylight_detector, diamond_pickaxe, diamond_axe, diamond_shovel, diamond_hoe, diamond_sword, diamond_spear, diamond_helmet, diamond_chestplate, diamond_leggings, diamond_boots
  • Villager trades: all disabled (every profession + wandering trader)
  • Fishing: treasure catches no longer come pre-enchanted
  • Blocked entities: iron_golem, piglin, piglin_brute

The Storylines

Start — no prerequisites Current

Founding the Camp

This is where Commonhold Rise actually begins — not with a quest, but with shelter. The ship is gone, the Crown's blessing is a name on a scroll, and what's left is a stretch of unclaimed coastline and whatever the group can build with their own hands. Before anyone touches a portal or wires a redstone contraption, New Haven needs walls: a personal space for every member within the base boundary, working crop farms, livestock pens, and a common store everyone can draw from. It's slow, deliberate work on purpose — everything the season builds toward rests on the ground laid down right here.

Pass Criteria

  • Every member has claimed and built a personal space within the base boundary
  • Working farms established for all 6 crops: wheat, carrots, potatoes, beetroot, melon, and pumpkin
  • At least two livestock pens are built and stocked
  • A common storage location is designated and accessible to the whole group

Unlocks

  • Contact made with the nearby village

Leads to: Breaking Bread

Requires: Founding the Camp

Breaking Bread

The nearest village wants nothing to do with New Haven. Too many wandering bands have come through before with more interest in scavenging than settling, and right now the group looks exactly like one of them. Earning actual trust means showing up and doing something useful without asking for anything back first: a real road connecting the two settlements, and enough reinforcement — walls, lighting, whatever it takes — that the village stops losing its own people to mobs at night. Only once that work is visible and finished does anyone there warm up to New Haven at all — enough, at least, to let a cartographer start pointing the way toward other settlements. Actually trading with this village is a different matter; that trust gets earned later, the hard way.

Pass Criteria

  • A trade route (road) is built connecting the base to the nearby village
  • The village is reinforced against mob attacks (walls, lighting, or equivalent defenses)
  • The village suffers no further villager losses to mobs once reinforcement is complete

Unlocks

  • The village's cartographer starts trading — village maps only; maps to mansions, monuments, and other dungeons still need their own unlock

Leads to: Anchor Point, Hall of Heroes

Requires: Breaking Bread

Hall of Heroes

Every village New Haven backs from here on is about to get tested by fire, and someone decides those wins shouldn't just be a story people tell each other afterward. The group raises a proper Hall back at the original settlement — plaques, records, whatever passes for a monument this early in the season. Nothing that happens at any other village counts toward anything until it's written down here first.

Pass Criteria

  • A Hall of Heroes is constructed at the original settlement

Unlocks

  • A place now exists to formally record raid victories — each entry notes when the raid happened, who took part (at least 4 people — not one or two players soloing it), and, for any village found by map, displays the actual map that led there. Required before any village's raid win counts toward its trade unlock.

Leads to: The First Stand, Grazing Rights, Feathered Roads, Wool and Frost, Pelts and Pines, Masons of the Dunes, Shipwreck & Treasure

Requires: Breaking Bread

Anchor Point

One road into the village isn't the ceiling — it's the proof of concept. With food already moving by cart, the group turns to the coastline: a real dock, built to actually take on heavier trade than a wagon can carry. Word spreads with the new traffic too — sailors passing through start talking, and some of that luck rubs off on New Haven's own fishing lines.

Pass Criteria

  • A functional dock area is built and connected to the trade route
  • At least one ship is placed in the dock

Unlocks

  • Fishing rods and bows can come pre-enchanted again; enchanted books still don't drop from fishing

Leads to: Shipwreck & Treasure

Requires: Trading on the Edge Story & Unlocks TBD

The Forge Reopens

Gear takes a beating faster than anyone expected, and there's no fixing a cracked pickaxe or a chipped sword by wishing at it. After six villages' worth of raids and a season of hauling trade goods, the toll is finally too much to ignore — the group can spare the hands and the iron to build out a proper smithy and actually staff it, which is what brings the anvil ban down. Nothing about docks or boats or anything else.

Pass Criteria

  • A working forge/smithy structure is built
  • Someone is designated to keep it staffed and running

Unlocks

  • Anvils usable again (repairing and renaming gear is back on the table)
Requires: Anchor Point and Hall of Heroes

Shipwreck & Treasure

The dock doesn't just bring traders in — it gives the group somewhere to actually launch from. Word of a wreck out past the reefs has been floating around since the first boat went out, and it's worth chasing: a hold full of salvage, and buried somewhere in the wreckage, a map that isn't like the others the cartographer sells. It only points to a general area, not a marked X, so it takes real digging — literal shovel work — to actually turn up the chest. Whatever's inside is worth the trouble: raw materials the whole group can put toward the same thing, rather than one player pocketing it and moving on — and worth writing down properly, same as every other win that's gone into the Hall.

Pass Criteria

  • A shipwreck is located and looted, recovering a Buried Treasure map
  • The map is followed to the buried treasure site and the chest is dug up
  • At least 4 people take part across the whole event, wreck to chest — see the Commonhold Ethos
  • The find is recorded in the Hall of Heroes — noting when it happened and who took part

Unlocks

  • Heart of the Sea usable
  • Conduit recipe usable
  • The cartographer's table gains the monument Explorer Map trade

Leads to: Atlantis Ahoy

Requires: Hall of Heroes

The First Stand

Bad Omen was never locked behind anything — any Illager Outpost the group finds gives up an Ominous Bottle, and there are always more outposts than raids the group actually needs to run. With the Hall standing and a bottle in hand, they turn it on the very first village they backed — on purpose, at full intensity, to prove the fortifications from Breaking Bread actually hold up under real pressure instead of just against whatever wandered by. Surviving it and getting the win written into the Hall is what finally convinces the village to start selling New Haven food again.

Pass Criteria

  • The original village is fully fortified against raids
  • The group survives one maximum-intensity raid together at the original village
  • The win is recorded in the Hall of Heroes — noting when it happened and who took part

Unlocks

  • Farmer trades restored

Leads to: Trading on the Edge

Requires: Hall of Heroes

Trading on the Edge

Six villages defended, six wins written into the Hall — word of that travels through backroads faster than New Haven ever expected, and the Wandering Traders finally approach. They've decided New Haven is worth dealing with, and they're ready to open their own trades for good. The catch is where they want to set up shop: demand for pale wood has spiked somewhere down their trade routes, and they want their headquarters built right on the edge of the nearest Pale Garden to keep the supply moving. Building it here is just the opening move — whatever's deeper in that Garden is a different problem for a different day.

Pass Criteria

  • A Wandering Trader waystation is built near a Pale Garden

Unlocks

  • Special Wandering Trader trades unlocked

Leads to: Woodland Mansion, The Forge Reopens

Requires: Trading on the Edge Story & Unlocks TBD

Woodland Mansion

A Woodland Mansion turns up somewhere out past the Dark Forest, deeper and darker than anything the group's raided so far — stacked with more Evokers than any single fight has thrown at them yet. Whatever's hiding in there matters to the bigger mystery Commonhold Rise exists to answer, and clearing it is what finally turns up a map worth following underground. Exactly how the group first hears about this place is still an open question — this whole entry point needs a real story, not just a placeholder connection to Trading on the Edge.

Two of the game's harder challenges land back-to-back here. The Mansion's map points to a Trial Chambers complex — clear it, and the Mace is finally in reach. From there it's a straight line to the Ancient City itself, guarded by wardens and worth raiding for the Silence trim alone. Clearing both is what finally earns the group leadership's sign-off to open the Nether — the first dimension gate the season lifts.

Unplanned Progression

Things that are real standing restrictions today but don't have a real story or milestone attached yet — parked here on purpose instead of forcing them onto a node that isn't ready. Future story planning and unlocks will be discussed and coordinated with the group, and decided by majority vote.

  • Pistons and dispensers. Banned since Founding the Camp. Used to hang off a "Witch's Hovel" idea that wasn't developed enough to commit to — cut for now. Needs a real replacement milestone.
  • Diamond gear. Tools, sword, spear, and armor all banned since Founding the Camp. Tools used to hang off Illager Outpost and the weapons/armor off a dedicated Diamond Gear node — neither exists in the tree anymore, so the whole diamond kit needs a new unlock milestone.
  • How the group first finds the Woodland Mansion. The node itself is fully built out (Pass Criteria, Unlocks, everything downstream), but the discovery hook — how they even learn it's out there — is currently just a placeholder pointing at Trading on the Edge. Needs its own story.
  • Comparator and Daylight Detector. Banned since Founding the Camp, same as the diamond kit — locked from day one, before the Nether even exists to get quartz from. The idea is that once The Nether Opens, quartz itself and everything built purely from Quartz Block stays fair game, but these two redstone components stay off-limits until a real milestone says otherwise.
