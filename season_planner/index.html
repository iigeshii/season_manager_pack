Commonhold Rise · the premise

Commonhold Rise

The Crown had called for settlers to a newly discovered land, and we were the crew who answered — not under a tyrant, but as a community, under one banner.

We arrive at the spawning settlement, where the court mage has graciously provided a point of Revival, such that none of us may ever truly die during our endeavors. We begin as the Crown once intended, just poorer for the journey. Nobody is a king. Nobody owns land. Nobody holds a title. We gather what the old world taught us and what little this shore provides — wood, stone, iron, food, shelter — and raise our first settlement. We call it New Haven.

Already, explorers speak of forests untouched, mountains holding ores nobody's named, deserts hiding ruins that aren't ours. One ruin, hundreds of kilometers from the next, carries the same carving: a circle ringed by seven marks.

Someone was here first. That's the mystery Commonhold Rise exists to answer.

The Commonhold Ethos

  • Nothing is automated. No iron farms, no book farms — what you have, you mined, farmed, or hunted yourself.
  • Everything is walked, ridden, or sailed. That's why the roads — and the small settlements along them — matter.
Year 0 · progression calendar

New Haven — 12-Month Progression

Each stage below tells the story of that arc, the explicit criteria the group has to clear to move on, and exactly what the pack enforces while you're in it. Months are approximate targets, paced to how the group is actually doing — not a fixed calendar.

Month ~1 Current

Founding the Camp

This is where Commonhold Rise actually begins — not with a quest, but with shelter. The ship is gone, the Crown's blessing is a name on a scroll, and what's left is a stretch of unclaimed coastline and whatever the group can build with their own hands. Before anyone touches a portal or wires a redstone contraption, the settlement needs walls: a personal space for every member within the base boundary, working crop farms, livestock pens, and a common store everyone can draw from. It's slow, deliberate work on purpose — everything the season builds toward rests on the ground laid down this month.

Pass Criteria

  • Every member has claimed and built a personal space within the base boundary
  • Working farms established for all 6 crops: wheat, carrots, potatoes, beetroot, melon, and pumpkin
  • At least two livestock pens are built and stocked
  • A common storage location is designated and accessible to the whole group

Enforcement

  • Nether portal blocked, End portal blocked
  • Items disallowed: hopper, hopper_minecart, piston, sticky_piston, dispenser, dropper, observer, slime, honey_block, elytra
  • Made uncraftable: blaze_powder, enchanting_table
  • Villager trades: all disabled (every profession + wandering trader)
  • Fishing: treasure catches no longer come pre-enchanted
  • Blocked entities: iron_golem, piglin, piglin_brute — despawned the instant they spawn
Month ~2

Trade & Trouble in the Woods

With the base standing, the group's attention turns outward for the first time — but only as far as the treeline. Word arrives that the Wandering Trader Guild wants a permanent outpost built in exchange for favorable terms, and closer to home, Pale Garden Creaking activity has gotten hard to ignore. Both threads run in parallel this month: raise the Guild's headquarters, and get to the bottom of whatever's animating those trees, all while the Nether and the End remain exactly as sealed as they were on day one.

Pass Criteria

  • Wandering Trader Guild HQ is fully built
  • The Pale Garden / Creaking mystery is investigated and resolved

Enforcement

  • Same standing portal lock + dimension bounce-back as Month 1 — no pack change needed to stay here
Month ~3

Era of the Illager

The Guild's opening trades point straight at an Illager Outpost nobody had mapped before. Raiding it is the easy part — the retaliation raid that follows is the real test of whether the base built in Month 1 can actually hold. Once the group has weathered that and the Outpost is cleared, the trail leads deeper: a Woodland Mansion, rumored to be hiding something that matters to the bigger mystery Commonhold Rise exists to answer.

Pass Criteria

  • Illager Outpost raided and held
  • The retaliation raid is survived
  • Woodland Mansion cleared, Ancient City map recovered

Enforcement

  • Same standing lock as Month 1 — a content/honor-system gate, not a new technical restriction
Month ~4

Trials & the Ancient City

Two of the game's harder challenges land back-to-back this month. The Mansion's map points to a Trial Chambers complex — clear it, and the Mace is finally in reach. From there it's a straight line to the Ancient City itself, guarded by wardens and worth raiding for the Silence trim alone. Clearing both is what finally earns the group leadership's sign-off to open the Nether — the first dimension gate the season lifts.

Pass Criteria

  • Trial Chambers cleared (Mace obtained)
  • Ancient City raided (Silence trim recovered)

Enforcement

  • Same standing lock, unchanged since Month 1
Month ~5

The Nether Opens / Cult of the Wither

The gate finally lifts — but only one of them. This month is about actually using the Nether once it's open: a proper hub, first contact with whatever built the shrines scattered through the wastes, and the Wither fight those shrines were clearly building toward. The End stays sealed throughout, and the Elytra question — raised back in Month 1 — is still sitting on the table, unresolved.

Pass Criteria

  • Nether hub established
  • Nether shrines uncovered and investigated
  • The Wither is killed

Enforcement

  • Admin removes minecraft:nether from DISABLED_DIMENSIONS (and its entry in PORTAL_IGNITERS), rebuilds, and redistributes the pack — there's no in-game toggle per dimension
  • End stays locked
  • Elytra stays in CLEANUP_ITEMS regardless — a standing rule, not month-gated
Month ~6

Road to El Porko

The Wither's death leaves a power vacuum among the Piglins, and the group moves to fill it before something worse does. All four Bastion Remnant variants are on the table this month — treasure, housing, stables, and mob — each one a small siege in its own right. Clearing them all is what finally makes the Nether highways safe enough to call settled, and cracks open netherite gear for the first time all season.

Pass Criteria

  • All 4 Bastion Remnant variants cleared

Enforcement

  • Same End lock as Month 5 — no pack change for this month
  • Piglins/piglin brutes are still despawned on spawn (standing rule from Month 1) — bastions will be undefended until that's revisited
Month ~7

Atlantis Ahoy

After five straight months of overland and underground pushes, the group finally looks at the water. A bottle found adrift points to a shipwreck, and the wreck points further — a treasure cave, and eventually a full Ocean Monument raid. It's a slower, exploration-heavy month by design, and one with no hard dependency on anything before or after it, which makes it the safest place in the whole calendar to absorb schedule slippage.

Pass Criteria

  • Shipwreck and treasure cave found and looted
  • Ocean Monument cleared

Enforcement

  • Same End lock — unchanged
Month ~8

Gibliria

Four unrelated sites — a desert Pyramid, a Jungle Temple, an abandoned Church, and a lone Igloo — all turn out to carry pieces of the same puzzle once the group starts comparing notes. Solving it, whatever "it" turns out to be, is the real milestone; looting the Flying City the clues eventually lead to is almost a formality by comparison. Getting this right is what finally authorizes the Stronghold hunt and puts an actual date on the dragon fight.

Pass Criteria

  • Clues gathered from the Pyramid, Jungle Temple, Church, and Igloo
  • The Gibliria puzzle is solved
  • The Flying City is looted

Enforcement

  • Same End lock — unchanged
Month ~9

Free the End

Everything since Month 4 has been building toward this. The Stronghold hunt is on, the last Eyes of Ender need gathering, and the Ender Dragon herself is the final boundary standing between the group and the outer End. Villager trading is still fully shut off across the board, same as it's been since Month 1; nothing about the dragon fight relaxes that. But killing her is what finally opens the End Gateways and makes the outer End reachable for the first time all season.

Pass Criteria

  • Stronghold located
  • Remaining Eyes of Ender gathered
  • Ender Dragon killed

Enforcement

  • Villager trading remains fully disabled (every profession + wandering trader) — same standing rule as Month 1, not month-gated
  • End lock lifts at the end of this month: admin removes minecraft:the_end from DISABLED_DIMENSIONS, rebuilds, redistributes
Month ~10

Twisty Edges

With the Gateways open, the outer End islands are finally worth the trip — chorus fruit, end cities, shulkers, all of it. Buried somewhere out there is the Mending Librarian, and rescuing her is this month's actual objective: a permanent, single-trade villager who can grant Mending even while normal villager trading everywhere else is still fully disabled. Her rescue is also what finally forces a real decision on the Elytra question that's been open since Month 1.

Pass Criteria

  • Outer End islands explored
  • Mending Librarian located and rescued

Enforcement

  • Rescue Villager feature: run /function season/summon_mending_librarian to spawn the permanent Mending Librarian (one trade, mending_librarian_trades.json) — the only unlock on this page that's a live in-game command instead of a pack rebuild
  • The Elytra policy decision (removing elytra from CLEANUP_ITEMS) still needs a rebuild once decided
Month ~11

The Path of St. Lorre, Pt. 1–2

No new dimension, no new gear tier — just the mystery itself, finally moving. A clue chain that starts in the Stronghold library runs through two full parts this month, each one narrowing down where "St. Lorre" and the seven-marks ruins actually connect. No standing rule lifts or tightens this month; it's a pure content push.

Pass Criteria

  • Stronghold library clue chain followed through both parts
  • Final clue location identified

Enforcement

  • No pack changes — standing rules (villager trades disabled, blaze powder/enchanting table recipe locks, fishing enchant removal, mob ban) continue unchanged
Month ~12

The Treasure of St. Lorre / Season Finale

The season closes on the mystery it opened with. Whatever's waiting at the end of the St. Lorre clue chain gets recovered, the seven-marks question gets an actual answer, and Commonhold Rise's first season wraps. What happens after that isn't automatic — it's a vote, requiring a supermajority of active members, in increments of four months at a time.

Pass Criteria

  • St. Lorre treasure recovered
  • Seven-marks mystery resolved

Enforcement

  • No pack changes

Notes

  • Permanent bans (elytra pending, hoppers, etc.) sit on top of every month below regardless of stage — each month's Enforcement block only calls out what's new or still active, not the full standing rule set already spelled out in Month 1.
  • Month 1 has no combat/exploration milestone on purpose — it's the only month where the criteria are about redstone tier and dimension access rather than an event gate, since there's no quest to fail yet.
  • If a month's raid/puzzle runs long, the safest place to absorb slippage is Month 7–8 (Atlantis/Gibliria have no dependency on anything before or after them).
