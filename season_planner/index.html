Commonhold Rise · the premise

Commonhold Rise

The Crown had called for settlers to a newly discovered land, and we were the crew who answered — not under a tyrant, but as a community, under one banner.

We arrive at the spawning settlement, where the court mage has graciously provided a point of Revival, such that none of us may ever truly die during our endeavors. We begin as the Crown once intended, just poorer for the journey. Nobody is a king. Nobody owns land. Nobody holds a title. We gather what the old world taught us and what little this shore provides — wood, stone, iron, food, shelter — and raise our first settlement. We call it New Haven.

Already, explorers speak of forests untouched, mountains holding ores nobody's named, deserts hiding ruins that aren't ours. One ruin, hundreds of kilometers from the next, carries the same carving: a circle ringed by seven marks.

Someone was here first. That's the mystery Commonhold Rise exists to answer.

The Commonhold Ethos

  • Nothing is automated. No iron farms, no book farms — what you have, you mined, farmed, or hunted yourself.
  • Everything is walked, ridden, or sailed. That's why the roads — and the small settlements along them — matter.
  • Some milestones belong to everyone. Where a node below says a find has to happen "as a group," that's not something the pack checks in code — it's a house rule the group enforces on its honor, the same as every other Pass Criteria on this page.

Start — no prerequisites Current

Founding the Camp

This is where Commonhold Rise actually begins — not with a quest, but with shelter. The ship is gone, the Crown's blessing is a name on a scroll, and what's left is a stretch of unclaimed coastline and whatever the group can build with their own hands. Before anyone touches a portal or wires a redstone contraption, the settlement needs walls: a personal space for every member within the base boundary, working crop farms, livestock pens, and a common store everyone can draw from. It's slow, deliberate work on purpose — everything the season builds toward rests on the ground laid down right here.

Pass Criteria

  • Every member has claimed and built a personal space within the base boundary
  • Working farms established for all 6 crops: wheat, carrots, potatoes, beetroot, melon, and pumpkin
  • At least two livestock pens are built and stocked
  • A common storage location is designated and accessible to the whole group

Unlocks

  • Contact made with the nearby village

Enforcement

  • Nether portal blocked, End portal blocked
  • Banned permanently (Commonhold Ethos, no milestone lifts these): hopper, hopper_minecart, dropper, observer
  • Banned until The Witch's Hovel is cleared: piston, dispenser
  • Banned pending a policy decision (no milestone defined yet): elytra, sticky_piston, slime, honey_block — sticky pistons need a slimeball to craft, so they stay locked with slime regardless of what happens at The Witch's Hovel
  • Banned until Anchor & Anvil is cleared: anvil, chipped_anvil, damaged_anvil
  • Banned until The Shipwreck is cleared: heart_of_the_sea
  • Made uncraftable: blaze_powder, enchanting_table
  • Made uncraftable until Buried Treasure is found: conduit — locked on top of the heart_of_the_sea ban, so a stray Heart of the Sea can't shortcut it
  • Made uncraftable pending a policy decision — see Diamond Tools / Diamond Gear below: diamond_pickaxe, diamond_axe, diamond_shovel, diamond_hoe, diamond_sword, diamond_helmet, diamond_chestplate, diamond_leggings, diamond_boots — diamonds themselves can still be mined and held, found/looted diamond gear is unaffected
  • Villager trades: all disabled (every profession + wandering trader)
  • Fishing: treasure catches no longer come pre-enchanted
  • Blocked entities: iron_golem, piglin, piglin_brute — despawned the instant they spawn

Leads to: Breaking Bread, The Witch's Hovel

Requires: Founding the Camp

Breaking Bread

The nearest village wants nothing to do with New Haven. Too many wandering bands have come through before with more interest in scavenging than settling, and right now the group looks exactly like one of them. Earning actual trust means showing up and doing something useful without asking for anything back first: a real road connecting the two settlements, and enough reinforcement — walls, lighting, whatever it takes — that the village stops losing its own people to mobs at night. Only once that work is visible and finished does anyone there warm up to the idea of selling New Haven anything at all.

Pass Criteria

  • A trade route (road) is built connecting the base to the nearby village
  • The village is reinforced against mob attacks (walls, lighting, or equivalent defenses)
  • The village suffers no further villager losses to mobs once reinforcement is complete

Unlocks

  • Villagers now trade food for emeralds (farmer trades restored)
  • The village's cartographer starts trading again too — village maps only; maps to mansions, monuments, and other dungeons still need their own unlock

Leads to: Anchor & Anvil, Grazing Rights, Feathered Roads, Wool and Frost, Pelts and Pines, Masons of the Dunes, Trade & Trouble in the Woods

Requires: Breaking Bread

Grazing Rights

The road to the first village proved the concept; now it's just a matter of building more of them. Word travels fast once a cartographer starts asking around, and it isn't long before a second settlement turns up out on the open plains — more livestock than the first village ever had room for. A trade route out there is what finally gets New Haven access to more than food: hides, meat, the whole butchering trade the first village never had a use for.

Pass Criteria

  • A cartographer sells the Plains Village map
  • A trade route is built to the plains village found

Unlocks

  • Butcher trades restored
Requires: Breaking Bread

Feathered Roads

Past the plains the land opens into savanna — acacia scrub, exposed hills, and a village that's clearly had to defend itself more than most. Their fletcher's been busy. A trade route out here is what finally gets New Haven real arrows and ranged supplies, instead of whatever the group's managed to scrape together on their own.

Pass Criteria

  • A cartographer sells the Savanna Village map
  • A trade route is built to the savanna village found

Unlocks

  • Fletcher trades restored
Requires: Breaking Bread

Wool and Frost

Further out, in terrain the group hasn't bothered mapping yet, is a village buried in snow — practical architecture, warm colors everywhere, and a shepherd with more wool on hand than anyone knows what to do with. A trade route out here means dye, wool, and the whole textile side of things New Haven's been improvising without all season.

Pass Criteria

  • A cartographer sells the Snowy Village map
  • A trade route is built to the snowy village found

Unlocks

  • Shepherd trades restored
Requires: Breaking Bread

Pelts and Pines

The taiga village turns up in thick pine forest, wolves circling the treeline, and a leatherworker who's clearly been busy. A trade route out here is what finally gets New Haven proper leather goods — the kind the group's been making do without since the season started.

Pass Criteria

  • A cartographer sells the Taiga Village map
  • A trade route is built to the taiga village found

Unlocks

  • Leatherworker trades restored
Requires: Breaking Bread

Masons of the Dunes

Out past the savanna, sandstone walls rise from the dunes — a desert village, and a stone mason with more chiseled sandstone and quartz on hand than New Haven's own builders have ever had the patience for. A trade route out here means real stonework, not just whatever the group's managed to quarry by hand.

Pass Criteria

  • A cartographer sells the Desert Village map
  • A trade route is built to the desert village found

Unlocks

  • Stone Mason trades restored
Requires: Breaking Bread

Anchor & Anvil

One road into the village isn't the ceiling — it's the proof of concept. With food already moving by cart, the group turns to the coastline: a real dock, built to actually take on heavier trade than a wagon can carry. Getting it running means putting an anvil to work for the first time all season, which means the standing anvil ban has to come down before the dock is worth anything at all. Word spreads with the new traffic too — sailors passing through start talking, and some of that luck rubs off on New Haven's own fishing lines.

Pass Criteria

  • A functional dock area is built and connected to the trade route
  • At least one boat is in active use for trade out of the dock

Unlocks

  • Anvils usable again (repairing gear is back on the table)
  • Fishing rods and bows can come pre-enchanted again; enchanted books still don't drop from fishing

Leads to: The Shipwreck

Requires: Anchor & Anvil

The Shipwreck

The dock doesn't just bring traders in — it gives the group somewhere to actually launch from. Word of a wreck out past the reefs has been floating around since the first boat went out, and it turns out to be worth chasing: a hold full of salvage, and buried somewhere in the wreckage, a map that isn't like the others the cartographer sells. Whatever's marked on it is worth more than the wreck itself.

Pass Criteria

  • A shipwreck is located and looted, recovering a Buried Treasure map
  • At least half the active group (or 4 people, whichever is fewer) is present when the wreck is cleared — see the Commonhold Ethos

Unlocks

  • Heart of the Sea usable

Leads to: Buried Treasure

Requires: The Shipwreck

Buried Treasure

The map only points to a general area, not a marked X, so it takes real digging — literal shovel work — to actually turn up the chest. Whatever's inside is worth the trouble: raw materials the whole group can put toward the same thing, rather than one player pocketing it and moving on.

Pass Criteria

  • The map is followed to the buried treasure site and the chest is dug up
  • At least half the active group (or 4 people, whichever is fewer) is present when the chest is opened — see the Commonhold Ethos

Unlocks

  • Conduit recipe usable

Leads to: Atlantis Ahoy

Requires: Founding the Camp

The Witch's Hovel

Not every witch hut is harmless. This one's grown into something bigger and far better defended — a real complex, crawling with witches and whatever else they've got guarding the place, not a lone hut with a cauldron out front. Somewhere in the mess, alongside all the potion ingredients, are pistons and dispensers the witches have clearly been using themselves. Clearing the place out is one thing; hauling a working piston and a working dispenser back to base is what actually lets the rest of the group study them up close — which is what it takes before anyone trusts the tech enough to build with it.

Pass Criteria

  • The Witch's Hovel is located
  • Every witch (and anything else guarding the place) is defeated and the structure is held
  • A piston is recovered and brought back to base for inspection
  • A dispenser is recovered and brought back to base for inspection

Unlocks

  • Pistons usable
  • Dispensers usable
Requires: Breaking Bread

Trade & Trouble in the Woods

With the base standing, the group's attention turns outward for the first time — but only as far as the treeline. Word arrives that the Wandering Trader Guild wants a permanent outpost built in exchange for favorable terms, and closer to home, Pale Garden Creaking activity has gotten hard to ignore. Both threads run in parallel: raise the Guild's headquarters, and get to the bottom of whatever's animating those trees, all while the Nether and the End remain exactly as sealed as they were on day one.

Pass Criteria

  • Wandering Trader Guild HQ is fully built
  • The Pale Garden / Creaking mystery is investigated and resolved

Unlocks

  • Illager Outpost location known

Leads to: Illager Outpost

Requires: Trade & Trouble in the Woods

Illager Outpost

The Guild's opening trades point straight at an Illager Outpost nobody had mapped before. Raiding it is the easy part — the retaliation raid that follows is the real test of whether the base built back at Founding the Camp can actually hold. Weathering both is what actually teaches the group how the Illagers operate: how to read the signs of a coming raid, how to provoke one on purpose, and what's worth fighting for when Evokers show up in the waves. Word of it also reaches the cartographer back in the village — enough to earn the group a very specific map, the kind that points to something bigger and deeper in the woods.

Pass Criteria

  • Illager Outpost raided and held
  • The retaliation raid is survived

Unlocks

  • Bad Omen obtainable (Ominous Bottles) — the group can trigger a raid on the village on demand
  • Evokers now show up in raid waves — Totems of Undying obtainable
  • The cartographer's table gains the mansion Explorer Map trade (Master tier moved down to no trade-leveling required); Woodland Mansion location known once bought

Leads to: Diamond Tools, Woodland Mansion

Requires: Illager Outpost

Woodland Mansion

The Outpost was a skirmish; the Mansion is the real dungeon. Deeper, darker, and stacked with more Evokers than any raid wave — whatever's hiding in there matters to the bigger mystery Commonhold Rise exists to answer, and clearing it is what finally turns up a map worth following underground.

Pass Criteria

  • Woodland Mansion cleared, Ancient City map recovered

Unlocks

  • Trial Chambers and Ancient City locations known

Leads to: Trials & the Ancient City

Requires: Illager Outpost Unlock TBD

Diamond Tools

Diamond tools are locked from the very start — every recipe exists, but each one now needs an ingredient nobody can ever obtain in survival. Diamonds themselves are still perfectly mineable and stackable, they just can't be turned into anything yet. What actually lifts the lock hasn't been decided; sitting this node off the Illager Outpost is a starting guess, not a decision.

Pass Criteria

  • Not yet defined — the unlock event hasn't been decided

Unlocks

  • Diamond pickaxe, axe, shovel, and hoe become craftable again (once an unlock event is chosen)
Requires: Woodland Mansion

Trials & the Ancient City

Two of the game's harder challenges land back-to-back here. The Mansion's map points to a Trial Chambers complex — clear it, and the Mace is finally in reach. From there it's a straight line to the Ancient City itself, guarded by wardens and worth raiding for the Silence trim alone. Clearing both is what finally earns the group leadership's sign-off to open the Nether — the first dimension gate the season lifts.

Pass Criteria

  • Trial Chambers cleared (Mace obtained)
  • Ancient City raided (Silence trim recovered)

Unlocks

  • Nether access approved; portal lock lifts for the Nether

Leads to: Diamond Gear, The Nether Opens

Requires: Trials & the Ancient City Unlock TBD

Diamond Gear

Same situation as diamond tools: the diamond sword and every piece of diamond armor are locked from the start, each recipe needing an ingredient nobody can obtain in survival. Parking the unlock behind the Ancient City raid is a starting guess — a harder, later milestone than tools, since combat gear matters more once real threats show up — but nothing's decided yet.

Pass Criteria

  • Not yet defined — the unlock event hasn't been decided

Unlocks

  • Diamond helmet, chestplate, leggings, boots, and sword become craftable again (once an unlock event is chosen)
Requires: Trials & the Ancient City

The Nether Opens / Cult of the Wither

The gate finally lifts — but only one of them. This stage is about actually using the Nether once it's open: a proper hub, first contact with whatever built the shrines scattered through the wastes, and the Wither fight those shrines were clearly building toward. The End stays sealed throughout, and the Elytra question — raised back at Founding the Camp — is still sitting on the table, unresolved.

Pass Criteria

  • Nether hub established
  • Nether shrines uncovered and investigated
  • The Wither is killed

Unlocks

  • Bastion Remnant locations known

Leads to: Road to El Porko

Requires: The Nether Opens

Road to El Porko

The Wither's death leaves a power vacuum among the Piglins, and the group moves to fill it before something worse does. All four Bastion Remnant variants are on the table — treasure, housing, stables, and mob — each one a small siege in its own right. Clearing them all is what finally makes the Nether highways safe enough to call settled, and cracks open netherite gear for the first time all season.

Pass Criteria

  • All 4 Bastion Remnant variants cleared

Unlocks

  • Netherite gear now obtainable; Nether highways declared safe

Leads to: Free the End

Requires: Buried Treasure

Atlantis Ahoy

With a Conduit finally in hand, the water stops being something to avoid and starts being worth diving into on purpose. A second wreck — a different one, further out — turns up a lead on a treasure cave, and from there it's a straight shot to a full Ocean Monument raid. Having a Conduit already running is the difference between fighting Elder Guardians and just surviving them.

Pass Criteria

  • Shipwreck and treasure cave found and looted
  • Ocean Monument cleared

Unlocks

  • Guardian-farm policy decided; Gibliria clue trail begins

Leads to: Gibliria

Requires: Atlantis Ahoy

Gibliria

Four unrelated sites — a desert Pyramid, a Jungle Temple, an abandoned Church, and a lone Igloo — all turn out to carry pieces of the same puzzle once the group starts comparing notes. Solving it, whatever "it" turns out to be, is the real milestone; looting the Flying City the clues eventually lead to is almost a formality by comparison. Getting this right is what finally authorizes the Stronghold hunt and puts an actual date on the dragon fight.

Pass Criteria

  • Clues gathered from the Pyramid, Jungle Temple, Church, and Igloo
  • The Gibliria puzzle is solved
  • The Flying City is looted

Unlocks

  • Stronghold hunt authorized; dragon fight scheduled

Leads to: Free the End

Requires: Road to El Porko and Gibliria

Free the End

This is where the Nether branch and the exploration branch actually converge. The Stronghold hunt is on, the last Eyes of Ender need gathering, and the Ender Dragon herself is the final boundary standing between the group and the outer End. Villager trading is still fully shut off across the board, same as it's been since Founding the Camp; nothing about the dragon fight relaxes that. But killing her is what finally opens the End Gateways and makes the outer End reachable for the first time all season.

Pass Criteria

  • Stronghold located
  • Remaining Eyes of Ender gathered
  • Ender Dragon killed

Unlocks

  • End Gateways open; outer End now reachable

Leads to: Twisty Edges, The Path of St. Lorre

Requires: Free the End

Twisty Edges

With the Gateways open, the outer End islands are finally worth the trip — chorus fruit, end cities, shulkers, all of it. Buried somewhere out there is the Mending Librarian, and rescuing her is the actual objective: a permanent, single-trade villager who can grant Mending even while normal villager trading everywhere else is still fully disabled. Her rescue is also what finally forces a real decision on the Elytra question that's been open since Founding the Camp.

Pass Criteria

  • Outer End islands explored
  • Mending Librarian located and rescued

Unlocks

  • Mending now available — run /function season/summon_mending_librarian to spawn the permanent Mending Librarian (the only unlock on this page that's a live in-game command instead of a configuration update)
  • Elytra policy decided

Leads to: Season Finale

Requires: Free the End

The Path of St. Lorre, Pt. 1–2

No new dimension, no new gear tier — just the mystery itself, finally moving. A clue chain that starts in the Stronghold library runs through two full parts, each one narrowing down where "St. Lorre" and the seven-marks ruins actually connect. No standing rule lifts or tightens here; it's a pure content push, running in parallel with Twisty Edges rather than blocking or being blocked by it.

Pass Criteria

  • Stronghold library clue chain followed through both parts
  • Final clue location identified

Unlocks

  • St. Lorre treasure location known

Leads to: Season Finale

Requires: Twisty Edges and The Path of St. Lorre

The Treasure of St. Lorre / Season Finale

The season closes on the mystery it opened with. Whatever's waiting at the end of the St. Lorre clue chain gets recovered, the seven-marks question gets an actual answer, and Commonhold Rise's first season wraps. What happens after that isn't automatic — it's a vote, requiring a supermajority of active members, in increments of four months at a time.

Pass Criteria

  • St. Lorre treasure recovered
  • Seven-marks mystery resolved

Unlocks

  • Extension vote triggered (4-month increments, supermajority required)

Notes

  • Permanent bans (hoppers, droppers, observers, etc.) sit on top of every branch below regardless of what's been cleared — each node's Enforcement block only calls out what's new or still active there, not the full standing rule set already spelled out at Founding the Camp.
  • Founding the Camp has no combat/exploration milestone on purpose — it's the only node where the criteria are about redstone tier and dimension access rather than an event gate, since there's no quest to fail yet.
  • Once its prerequisites (The Shipwreck, Buried Treasure) are cleared, Atlantis Ahoy and Gibliria are the safest branch to pick up whenever the group has downtime — nothing else in the tree depends on them being done at any particular time.
  • Diamond Tools and Diamond Gear are marked "Not Yet Enforced" on purpose — they're placeholders for a real decision, not restrictions that exist today.
